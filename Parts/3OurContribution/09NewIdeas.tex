\section{Ideas for New Alternative Games}
The first part of our contribution describes, evaluates, and presents the background of six alternative game concepts invented during our prestudy work. In chapter \ref{chap:5}, we described what an alternative game is, which helped us see the possibilities and variations within the space. By tweaking one of the six characteristics, we were able to come up with new ideas that we otherwise would not have considered. Chapter \ref{chap:6} gave us an overview of existing alternative games. This research showed us examples of the current possibilities and stimulated our fantasy to invent even more ideas. Finally, in chapter \ref{chap:8}, we studied how to create an enjoyable game that helped us further develop our concepts. The work we did in the prestudy, especially chapters \ref{chap:5} and \ref{chap:8}, will also come in handy later when we choose which concept we will continue to work on.

After outlining and evaluating the six ideas, the model defined in our prestudy will be used to compare them. This comparison will help to describe the differences between the games and why they characterize as alternative games.

\subsection{Background}
Our main motivating factor for choosing this specific task was the opportunity to create an application with a socially beneficial purpose. The concepts should preferably introduce something valuable to society and explore the genre of alternative games. Also, the goal was to make the application available to as many users as possible. Based on these factors, we decided early that the game had to be based on the mobile platform. Consequently, the concepts described later in this chapter are invented, to various degrees, with this in mind. Over three billion people own a smartphone today \citep{odea_smartphone_2020} and the vast majority of these people use either iOS or Android \citep{statcounter_mobile_2020}. By targeting the smartphone market and these two operating systems, we can reach a lot of potential users. Besides, smartphones have a variety of sensors that can be used in alternative games.

Another important consideration that we kept in mind during the invention process was that the concept needed to be easy to test. The application’s testability is essential when we are later developing the chosen concept in our master thesis. We want to find out how people are using the application and whether or not they are enjoying it.

Even though these restrictions limit the possibility of concepts that can be considered, we felt it improved our ability to be creative and invent new ideas. Early on, before settling on these restrictions, the scope of alternative games was too big, making it hard to come up with anything at all.
