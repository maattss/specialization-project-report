\section{Conclusion}
\label{chap:conclusion}


This project’s research goal was to \textit{develop one or more game concepts for an alternative game that has a socially beneficial effect, and that can be developed, explored, and tested}. To design such a game, we decomposed the goal into four research questions in Section \ref{sec:research_questions}.

\textbf{RQ1:} \textit{What makes a game engaging?} \\
Designing games are a delicate process. The literature review in chapters \ref{chap:designanenjoyable}-\ref{chap:rewardsystems}, discuss the many considerations designers should be aware of when designing enjoyable games. 
\citet{malone_what_1980} study on \textit{What Makes Things Fun to Learn} presents three important elements in learning games: \textit{Challenge}, \textit{Fantasy}, and \textit{Curiosity}. 

\citet{sweetser_gameflow_2005} study on Gameflow suggest that there are eight elements that can make games enjoyable: concentration, challenge, skills, control, clear goals, feedback, immersion, and social interaction. Each of these includes a goal and a set of criteria that can be used when developing games. Both \citet{malone_what_1980} and \citet{sweetser_gameflow_2005} share several game design principles, such as the importance of grabbing attention, including clear goals, appropriate difficulty, and constructive feedback.

\citet{hamari_player_2014} study on player types reviews the multitude of player type definitions discussed in the reviewed literature. \citeauthor{hamari_player_2014} synthesizes the findings into seven dimensions of player motivations: Achievement, Sociability, Exploration, Domination, Immersion, Gaming Intensity and skill, and In-game demographics. Out of the seven, Achievement and Sociability seem to be the most recurring motivations in the literature. 

\citet{wang_game_2011} study on reward systems give an overview of how reward systems are used in games to create positive player experiences. The article gave insight into different forms of rewards, reward characteristics. Based on previous research, \citeauthor{wang_game_2011} proposed eight forms of rewards used in games to create positive experiences for the players: \textit{Score systems}, \textit{Experience points reward systems}, \textit{Item granting system rewards}, \textit{Resources}, \textit{Achievement systems}, \textit{Feedback messages}, \textit{Unlocking mechanisms}, and \textit{Plot animations and pictures}. \citeauthor{wang_game_2011}  also proposes seven considerations for designing reward systems: \textit{Life Constraint}, \textit{Create autotelic experiences}, \textit{Balance}, \textit{Uncertainty and secrecy}, \textit{Accumulated vs. instant feedback}, \textit{Social purposes}, and \textit{Physical world activities}.

The literature reviewed highlights important considerations for designing enjoyable games. The various game design principles, player motivations, and reward types are essential to understand and include when designing enjoyable games. 

\textbf{RQ2:} \textit{What is an alternative game?}\\
To define the expression \textit{alternative games}, we took basis in the definitions of alternative: "\textit{Different from the usual or conventional.}" \citep{merriam-webster_alternative_2020}. From the literature search, we found that alternative games deviate from ordinary games; however, the characteristic of these deviations was very individual. We propose six game characteristic categories that make games alternative: \textit{Input}, \textit{Output}, \textit{Target group}, \textit{Game genre}, \textit{Domain}, and \textit{Interaction}. We also defined alternative games as: 
 “\textit{games that deviate from ordinary games in one or more of the game characteristics categories.}”

\textbf{RQ3:} \textit{How can alternative games be categorized and conceptualized?}\\
The definition of alternative games can be used to categorize and conceptualize alternative games. The study of existing games helped us create the six game characteristics categories that make games deviate from ordinary games. Existing games can be categorized as alternative using our model. If a game has one or more of the game characteristics in the six categories, it is an alternative game. Furthermore, our model provides a structured approach for conceptualizing new game concepts. Game creators can tweak the game characteristics within the categories to develop new alternative game concepts. The full model (see Appendix \ref{appendix:complete_model}) only contains the game characteristics we have studied and discovered; thus, it is important to point out that there might exist additional characteristics or characteristics that have yet to be developed. As technology moves forward, it is reasonable to assume that new categories and characteristics can emerge.

\textbf{RQ4:} \textit{Which alternative game concepts can be developed with existing technology that has a socially beneficial purpose?}\\
Our alternative game model was used as a structured approach to developing six new alternative game concepts presented in Chapter \ref{chap:ideas}. The concepts were all intended for the mobile platform because smartphones are highly available and portable; thus, easier to test as a socially beneficial concept. The concepts were evaluated on different criteria, such as testability, feasibility, scope, and degree of social benefits. Out of the six, Hover stood out as a game that could reach out to many players. Additionally, Hover’s evaluation suggests that the concept is feasible to develop, test, and evaluate within the scope of our master thesis. 

Hover is a pervasive game concept that encourages and rewards players for doing activities at specific places in the real world. The places are predefined locations that can be categorized under the domains of Education, Health, Culture, and Social. Players can post their activities to get rewards, such as experience points and badges. Additionally, players have in-game characters that develop according to their level of activity. The game also includes social features, such as interaction through likes and comments on activity posts. Another social feature is the leaderboard and the statistics, which players can use to compare themself to friends.

Hover’s social benefits come from motivating people to improve health, mental capabilities, socialize, and more. If the game concept is successful, it is valuable for both society and the individual. The game will be developed using the cross-platform framework React Native. When using such a framework, both Android and iOS users can be included in testing. React Native is a mature and well-documented framework with rich features and many supporting libraries which enable rapid development.
